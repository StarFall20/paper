# Round eleven: transformation-group collision audit

## New nearest neighbor

The search found *Latent utility and permutation invariance: A revealed
preference approach* (Journal of Econometrics, 2024,
[doi:10.1016/j.jeconom.2024.105844](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2024.105844)).
It uses a class of transformations \(\mathcal G\) under which latent utility
or unobservable distributions are invariant and differences out unobservables
to obtain identification inequalities. This is materially closer to the
transformation language than the earlier literature boundary.

Other nearby work includes [Extreme values, invariance and choice
probabilities](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trb.2013.10.014), which studies
invariance of achieved utility distributions across selected alternatives, and
[tests of rationality, continuity and symmetry in health-care DCEs](https://doi.org/10.1080/135048501750041312),
which use split-sample task-order changes to test behavioral assumptions.

## Collision decision

Do not claim that the present paper is the first use of a transformation group,
permutation invariance, or a symmetry contrast in choice research. That claim
would be too broad. Keep the primary claim at the level of an experimental
specification audit for an observed attribute basis.

The distinction is substantive:

| Dimension | Latent utility and permutation invariance | Present parity-fibre design |
|---|---|---|
| Transformation target | Latent utilities, unobservable distributions, or alternative permutations | Observed attribute profiles inside a candidate-preserving utility fibre |
| Main purpose | Partial identification and differencing out unobservables | Falsifying an insufficient deterministic utility basis |
| Menu condition | Invariance conditions on latent objects and feasibility sets | Complete candidate menu vector is held fixed by construction |
| Estimand | Identification inequalities for latent utility indices | Odd choice-probability response; even response is a processing diagnostic |
| Experimental role | Revealed-preference transformation argument | Pre-outcome DCE task design with one-member random assignment and cluster sign flips |
| Main limitation | Depends on latent invariance assumptions | Depends on a nontrivial observed fibre and declared even/odd nuisance library |

The parity construction can be described as a \(\mathbb Z_2\) special case for
notation, but the manuscript should not market it as a general group-invariance
theory. The independent value lies in choosing an observed attribute orbit that
keeps the candidate's menu prediction unchanged, then using the orbit's odd
component to test a candidate specification. The 2024 paper does not provide
that DCE instrument, menu-preserving task construction, calibration workflow,
or information criterion.

## Implication for the innovation claim

The safe novelty sentence is:

> We develop a candidate-preserving DCE specification audit that uses a
> reflection pair in observed attribute space. It holds the candidate's full
> menu prediction fixed, removes a declared even distance/scale component by
> construction, and tests the retained odd choice response for an omitted
> utility direction.

The unsafe sentence is “we introduce a general transformation-invariance test
for discrete choice models.” It should be removed.

## Required literature controls

The introduction and related-work section should cite the 2024 permutation
invariance paper, the extreme-value invariance paper, and the health-care
symmetry experiment. The paper should state that its transformation is an
observed design device, not a latent identification theorem. The empirical
protocol should retain the calibration pilot, semantic screening, and
full-menu preservation conditions because these are the points that separate
the method from ordinary label permutations or order effects.
