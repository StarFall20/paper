# Journal of Choice Modelling revision plan

## Main research question

Can a fitted candidate basis that coarsens the raw attribute space be tested on
new choice tasks that it declares equivalent? The nuisance-balanced utility-
fibre audit answers this question by constructing reflected task pairs with the
same full menu utility vector, the same declared comparison-complexity
signature, and different raw decompositions. The primary test is not run when
the feasible fibre is a singleton.

This is the only new object in the main paper. Random Forest, XGBoost, Mixed
Logit, latent-class MNL, and process models are comparison or negative-control
tools. They do not form separate contribution claims.

## Contribution statement

The paper develops a candidate-conditioned exchangeability test for discrete
choice specification. A development fold freezes the candidate basis. A
predeclared reflection preserves every component of the candidate menu vector
and the declared nuisance signature. A one-member-per-pair assignment
prevents respondents from seeing both members. Respondent-cluster sign flips
provide the finite-sample reference under the candidate null. The estimand is
a choice-probability relation on a declared nontrivial task fibre.

The paper does not claim a universal MNL misspecification test, a new
invariance axiom, a unique omitted term, or global model correctness.

## Round-eight method upgrade

The preferred refinement is nuisance-orthogonal utility-fibre testing. For a
set of candidate-preserving task pairs, estimate the local omitted-utility
signal and the tangent directions generated by scale, geometry, framing, and
order. Select a contrast in the weighted null space of the nuisance tangents.
This turns the zero-gap and geometry controls into one declared design
criterion. It is distinct from D-optimal parameter design and raw model
discrimination because the target is the residual candidate violation after
nuisance projection. The proof-of-concept and its operating boundary are in
`manuscript/nuisance_orthogonal_fibre_note.md`.

The upgrade remains conditional until it passes out-of-library nuisance
stress tests and an empirical one-member-per-pair supplement. The main paper
must report the signal-loss trade-off explicitly: in the current planning run,
projection lowers nuisance false rejection while also lowering power against a
weak omitted signal.

The direction now fits the journal's listed **Standalone Technical
Contributions to Choice Modelling** call: the contribution is a compact method
for designing and testing choice-model specifications. The current manuscript
should be reshaped as a short technical paper only after the empirical paired
task, LR/residual comparison, and power analysis are complete.

## Manuscript architecture

1. **Introduction.** Explain why a good holdout score does not test whether a
   coarsened utility library treats its own equivalent tasks consistently.
   State anchored BMST and the local interpretation boundary.
2. **Related literature.** Position BMST against assisted specification,
   axiomatic invariance, MNL misspecification tests, metamorphic testing,
   process models, and DCE design. State the exact delta in one paragraph.
3. **Candidate-fibre theory.** Define the basis, full menu difference vector,
   nontrivial-fibre screen, candidate-preserving transformation, probability
   relation, and estimand. State the softmax equivalence proposition.
4. **Randomized instrument.** Define the development/test split, one-member
   assignment, orientation coin, cluster score, sign-flip reference, order
   counterbalance, opt-out boundary, and failure conditions.
5. **Falsification-oriented design.** Select nuisance-balanced reflections by a
   declared movement budget. Report the candidate-menu balance, weighted
   weighted L1 balance, raw-load balance, and complexity-only arm before outcomes.
6. **Simulation testbed.** Retain the smart-medical-device generator as a
   controlled benchmark and add the coarsened-burden binary design. Compare
   anchored BMST with an observational LR test and an out-of-fold residual
   learner. Conditions include omitted decomposition, random taste, scale,
   order, and comparison complexity.
7. **Results.** Report size, power, repair, negative controls, design
   sensitivity, sample-size/task-count sensitivity, and the action for mixed
   signatures. Add the max-T fibre-violation profile to localize failures
   within the tested candidate support. Keep mechanism attribution bounded.
8. **Empirical instrument and data boundary.** Report the public-data audit and
   specify the paired-task supplement. If the supplement is not available,
   present the work as a simulation-validated method proposal rather than a
   completed empirical application.
9. **Discussion.** Explain the tested-domain value, limits of non-rejection,
   scale and process confounding, and the path to external validation.

## Role of the original Word manuscript

The original title, “When Does XGBoost Improve Choice Prediction for Smart
Medical Devices?”, and its lambda crossover are retained as an appendix
benchmark. The crossover is specific to the generator and cannot carry the
main novelty claim. The smart-device attributes remain useful because they
provide a transparent, multi-attribute testbed for the BMST relation.

## Evidence already available

- UFIT algebra and the randomization validity proposition are recorded in
  `manuscript/ufit_proposition.md`.
- The one-member assignment benchmark has 100 replications and 600
  respondents. Rejection is .05 under the additive null, .79 for omitted
  nonlinearity, .05 under random linear taste, and 1.00 for an omitted
  interaction.
- The negative-control benchmark has 100 replications. Rejection is .04 for
  the null, .68 for omitted nonlinearity, .03 for random taste, .23 for scale
  drift, and 1.00 for order effects.
- The constrained maximin design raises the weakest normalized probe score
  from 1.142 to 1.632. A repeated-shift unconstrained solution is retained as
  a failure boundary.
- The public-data audit found no existing dataset with the required
  candidate-preserving pair and one-member assignment. See
  `manuscript/public_data_pair_audit.md`.
- The anchored-fibre benchmark uses a coarsened burden (s=a_1+a_2) and an
  omitted decomposition coordinate (z=a_1-a_2). With 100 replications and
  400 respondents, the observational LR and residual-learning proxy reject at
  .04 and .04 under the omitted decomposition condition; anchored rejection is
  .83 for the zero-gap geometry-preserving arm and 1.00 for the nonzero-gap
  geometry-changing arm. Under complexity alone, the zero-gap and
  geometry-preserving rates are .02 and .07, while the geometry-changing
  nonzero-gap rate is .41. See `results/anchored_fibre_benchmark.csv`.
- The 60-replication sample-size planning run reports nonlinear power of .20,
  .42, and .70 at 150, 300, and 600 respondents with three focal pairs;
  interaction power is .90, 1.00, and 1.00. See
  `results/bmst_power_curve.csv`.

The frozen BMST outputs were re-run with the bundled analysis environment on
2026-10-06. Both benchmark CSVs matched the committed files byte for byte.

## Round-nine parity instrument

The preferred primary DCE instrument is now a reflection-symmetric fibre when
its attribute semantics permit it. The (+t/-t) central contrast removes even
geometry and scale effects algebraically, while the even component is reported
as a processing diagnostic. The round-eight nuisance-orthogonal projection is
retained for odd nuisance directions and for applications that cannot implement
the reflection. The empirical supplement must preserve all menu differences,
randomize one member per pair, counterbalance order and wording, and report
both parity components.

## Incremental diagnostic evidence

Add `results/parity_incremental_benchmark.csv` to the simulation section. The
benchmark uses the same respondent budget for a narrow observational design and
a reflected candidate-preserving supplement. Under the omitted direction,
observational LR and the residual proxy reject at .060 and .030, while parity
rejects at 1.000. Under an even scale nuisance, the corresponding rates are
.030, .060, and .050; the one-sided fibre comparator rejects at 1.000. Under
the combined condition, parity remains at 1.000 while the ordinary diagnostics
remain at .050 and .060. Present this as a planning comparison and repeat it
with the locked DCE generator before submission.

## Moderate-utility positioning

State the parity proposition for the broader (F(\Delta V/D)) class and cite

## Round-fourteen correction

The first five-task grid was retained only as a loose comparator after a direct
audit showed that its weighted L1 comparison-distance signature differed
between reflected members. The primary supplement uses the corrected
nuisance-balanced grid in `results/smart_device_nuisance_balanced_design.csv`.
The binary moderate-utility negative-control benchmark is in
`results/smart_device_complexity_balance_binary_benchmark.csv`; it shows
complexity-only rejection of .185 for the loose grid and .050 for the balanced
grid at 600 respondents. This correction is part of the identification logic,
not a cosmetic robustness check.

The profile diagnostic is implemented in
`analysis/smart_device_fibre_violation_profile.py` and
`results/smart_device_fibre_violation_profile.csv`. With a localized omitted
interaction, global power is .330, max-T profile power is .885, and the active
task is correctly selected in .990 of replications; null rejection is .035 and
.050. The profile is a secondary layer of the same fibre audit, not a second
theory.

The assignment audit now uses one orientation coin per respondent block, shared
across the five tasks. A corrected 150-respondent weak-signal comparison gives
.490 power for the ordinary grid and .870 for the fibre-optimal grid; the
combined omitted-plus-even-scale rates are .335 and .785. A separate random-
taste boundary control gives .085, .095, and .085 rejection at coefficient
standard deviations 0, .1, and .2. These results define the interpretation
boundary for the candidate relation; they do not identify a unique omitted
term. See `manuscript/novelty_audit_round16.md`.
