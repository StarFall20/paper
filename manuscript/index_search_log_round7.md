# Index search log for the anchored-fibre idea

**Search date:** 2026-10-06

## Scope

The search tested whether the proposed object already exists under a different
name. The target was the combination of a non-injective candidate utility
basis, a task transformation that preserves the full menu utility-difference
vector, one-member randomized assignment, a zero-gap scale anchor, and a
geometry-preserving comparison-complexity control.

## Closest papers read for the novelty boundary

1. [Ortelli et al. (2021), Assisted specification of discrete choice
   models](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jocm.2021.100285): combinatorial search over
   utility specifications; no experimental test relation.
2. [Fok and Paap (2025), New misspecification tests for multinomial logit
   models](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jocm.2024.100531): composite-likelihood and
   GMM tests for MNL/IIA misspecification; no candidate-equivalent task fibre.
3. [Pedersen et al. (2011), Cost-attribute inclusion in forced and unforced
   choices](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1755-5345(13)70044-7): random split design
   and equality-of-parameters test; the intervention changes the information
   shown to respondents rather than holding a fitted candidate relation fixed.
4. [Shubatt and Yang, Tradeoffs and Comparison
   Complexity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17578): value differences are divided
   by a feature/dominance complexity distance; the paper directly motivates
   the geometry control and predicts a zero-gap binary probability of one half
   under symmetric errors.
5. [Task-complexity effects in stated
   choice](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tre.2022.102744): estimates how task
   complexity changes error variability; it does not test a candidate's own
   equivalence relation.
6. [NIST metamorphic testing for simulation
   validation](https://www.nist.gov/publications/metamorphic-testing-continuum-verification-and-validation-simulation-models):
   transfers relation-based validation to simulation, without choice-specific
   utility fibres or randomized respondent assignment.

## Search conclusion

The direct-index check is incomplete at the access layer because Google Scholar
and Scopus pages were blocked or required authentication in the connected
environment. The cross-index search still located the closest conceptual
neighbors and tested the exact overlap terms. The claim supported by this log
is targeted novelty, not proof that no unpublished or inaccessible paper uses
the same construction. The final manuscript must state the search boundary and
avoid a universal “first ever” claim.
