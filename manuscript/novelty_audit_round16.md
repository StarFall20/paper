# Round sixteen: independence and assignment audit

## Independence test for the central idea

The paper's central object is one experimental design: a candidate-preserving
utility fibre, a nuisance signature, a respondent-block reflection, and an
odd/even response decomposition. Removing any one of these pieces changes the
estimand. The max-T profile and the task-ranking rule are outputs of this same
object. They are not additional theories.

The literature review assigns each borrowed ingredient a bounded role:

| Ingredient | Role in this paper | Claim excluded |
|---|---|---|
| Nonparametric and likelihood specification tests | comparator on observed support | a new universal specification test |
| Tradeoff-complexity measures | nuisance signature and negative control | a new cognitive-complexity theory |
| Latent-utility/permutation invariance | nearest theoretical boundary | a general transformation-group theorem |
| DCE design and ordering controls | implementation safeguards | a new efficiency theorem |
| Odd/even decomposition | estimand for the constructed reflection | identification of a unique omitted term |

The independent contribution is the pre-outcome construction of observed
attribute tasks that the frozen candidate maps to the same complete menu vector,
then balancing a declared processing signature before comparing responses. This
combination creates a testable relation in designed support. It is a bounded
observed-DCE contribution and depends on nontrivial fibres; a saturated basis
has singleton fibres.

## Assignment audit

The first simulation versions drew a new orientation inside each task while
using respondent-cluster sign flips. Those versions were discarded. The current
scripts draw one orientation coin per respondent block and share it across the
five tasks. The sign-flip reference now matches the assignment unit in the
profile, complexity, random-taste, and ordinary-grid benchmarks.

With 200 replications, 600 respondents, and 999 sign flips, the corrected
profile has global null rejection .035 and max-T null rejection .050. A
localized omitted direction gives global power .330, profile power .885, and
task localization .990. A diffuse direction gives 1.000 for both statistics.

## Heterogeneity boundary

The fibre is constructed from calibrated population coefficients. A negative
control draws respondent coefficients around that vector. Rejection is .085,
.095, and .085 at standard deviations 0, .1, and .2, compared with 1.000 for
the declared omitted direction. The small elevation at finite sample size is
reported as a boundary of the candidate relation. It cannot support a claim
that the method diagnoses a particular interaction when random coefficients are
outside the candidate.

## Decision rule

The paper should retain the design only if the final locked run preserves three
facts: nuisance balance brings the complexity-only rejection to the nominal
range; respondent-level randomization gives a correctly sized null; and the
profile is presented as a localization diagnostic. If any fact fails in the
human-data pilot, the result remains a relation audit and the utility-specific
interpretation is withheld.
