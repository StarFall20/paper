# Round seven audit: anchored utility fibres and the complexity objection

## Why the previous BMST claim needs tightening

The earlier BMST statement treated a rejection on a candidate-preserving task
fibre as evidence that the candidate basis was insufficient. That statement is
too broad. A change in observed choice probability can also reflect task
specific scale, presentation, or comparison difficulty. The paper must make
the experimental design carry part of the identification argument.

The revised object is an **anchored utility-fibre specification test**. It is
BMST restricted to non-injective candidate bases, with two design conditions:

1. a zero-candidate-difference anchor, where symmetric error scale cannot
   change the binary probability away from one half; and
2. a geometry-preserving fibre control, where the pairwise raw attribute
   differences that enter a comparison-complexity measure are held fixed.

The main claim is local: the design tests whether a coarsened candidate
representation is adequate on a declared fibre. A rejection outside the anchor
and geometry controls is a relation violation. It is not an automatic label
for a nonlinear term.

## Formal design

Let (r_j) be the raw attribute vector and let (b_j=g(r_j)) be the fitted
candidate basis. Define the full menu coordinate

\[
  \phi(x)=\{b_j(x)-b_k(x):j<k\}.
\]

A usable fibre requires at least two feasible task versions (x,x') with

\[
  \phi(x)=\phi(x')
\]

and a nonzero change in the raw attribute decomposition. A rank or null-space
screen is run before any outcomes are observed. If the candidate uses every raw
attribute separately and the feasible transformation set has no nonzero null
direction, the primary test is not run; the fibre is effectively a singleton.

For a composite candidate (s_j=a_{1j}+\kappa a_{2j}), a decomposition move is

\[
  (a_{1j},a_{2j})\mapsto(a_{1j}+\delta_j,
  a_{2j}-\delta_j/\kappa).
\]

It preserves (s_j) for every alternative. A common δ across alternatives also
preserves pairwise raw differences and serves as the geometry-preserving
control. Alternative-specific δ changes the raw comparison geometry while
preserving the candidate coordinate; it is used only in a predeclared
complexity arm.

For a binary task, the zero-gap anchor sets the candidate difference to

\[
  \Delta V=V_A-V_B=0.
\]

Under a symmetric error distribution, (P(A)=1/2) for every positive error
scale. A difference between paired decompositions at this anchor cannot be
generated by scale alone. It can still reflect asymmetric framing, order,
attention, or a utility component omitted by the candidate, so the paper will
call it a scale-invariant control rather than a full causal separator.

For a (J)-alternative menu, the transformation preserves the entire
φ-vector, not one selected pair. The zero-gap extension requires equal
candidate utilities for the complete menu and an exchangeable error structure;
the binary arm is the primary identification result and the multinomial arm is
a stated extension with its assumptions.

## The Yang--Shubatt objection and the response

Shubatt and Yang model binary choice probabilities as an increasing symmetric
function of a value difference divided by a comparison-complexity distance.
Their experiments show that more pronounced tradeoffs increase inconsistency
and error. This is a direct threat whenever a fibre move changes featurewise
similarity or dominance.

The primary fibre control therefore applies a common translation to every
alternative. It leaves each pairwise raw difference (r_j-r_k) unchanged, so
the similarity/dominance geometry in that mechanism is unchanged. The
alternative-specific decomposition arm deliberately changes that geometry and
is reported as a complexity response, not as evidence of utility
misspecification. The zero-gap anchor supplies an additional scale check for
the binary outcome.

Response time, self-reported confidence, and a predeclared raw-distance index
are collected in the DCE. They are validation variables for the complexity
arm, not post-hoc mechanism labels.

## Incremental simulation required for the paper

The revised benchmark has three data sources and three diagnostics:

1. **Observational support:** draw tasks close to a manifold where the raw
   decomposition coordinate is nearly constant. A candidate MNL, an enriched
   likelihood-ratio test, and an out-of-fold residual learner should have low
   power because the omitted direction is not observed.
2. **Geometry-preserving fibre supplement:** add one-member randomized pairs
   with the same candidate coordinate and the same pairwise raw geometry. A
   rejection here is evidence against the candidate relation, not against
   comparison complexity of the Shubatt--Yang form.
3. **Geometry-changing and zero-gap controls:** vary the raw decomposition at
   fixed candidate coordinate, with a zero-gap arm. These quantify the
   complexity channel and the scale-invariant anchor.

The comparison is deliberately asymmetric. The LR and residual-learning
methods are given the original observational support; the fibre instrument is
an additional design intervention. The paper will not claim that BMST
dominates a flexible model after both are given the same probe tasks. Its value
is that it creates a falsifiable task relation in a direction that ordinary
fit diagnostics may never observe.

The power study varies respondent count, focal-pair count, and decomposition
movement. It reports size at the additive null, power against an omitted
decomposition term, rejection under random taste, scale drift, order effects,
and the complexity-only control.

The first locked planning run uses 100 replications, 400 respondents, and 199
sign-flip draws. The observational LR and residual-learning proxy reject at
.04 and .04 under the omitted decomposition condition. The anchored test
rejects at .83 for the zero-gap geometry-preserving arm and 1.00 for the
nonzero-gap geometry-changing arm. Under complexity alone, the corresponding
rates are .06, .07, and .41; the zero-gap geometry-changing arm is .02. The
geometry-preserving null remains close to the declared size. These results
support the design logic while leaving the empirical DCE as a required gate.

The sample-size sensitivity run uses 60 replications per cell and 49
sign-flip draws. With three focal pairs, nonlinear power rises from .20 at 150
respondents to .42 at 300 and .70 at 600; interaction power is .90, 1.00, and
1.00 at the same sample sizes. The additive rejection rates are .08, .00, and
.03. The low number of planning replications and the reduced sign-flip count
make these figures suitable for design selection, not final inference.

## Literature and index-search record

The targeted search covered JOCM 2021--2025, exact phrase searches for
candidate-preserving and equivalent-utility choice tasks, and adjacent work on
misspecification, task complexity, metamorphic testing, and DCE design. The
closest boundaries are:

- Ortelli et al. (2021), assisted specification: searches utility libraries but
  does not create a candidate-implied task relation;
- Fok and Paap (2025), MNL misspecification tests: tests IIA-related
  misspecification through composite likelihood/GMM, not experimental fibres;
- Pedersen et al. (2011), random split on cost-attribute inclusion: tests a
  design intervention and equality of parameters, not candidate-equivalent
  task versions;
- Shubatt and Yang (2024--2026), comparison complexity: predicts errors from
  tradeoff geometry, which is why geometry must be controlled and measured;
- Swait--Louviere scale work and task-complexity DCE studies: establish the
  scale/process confounding that the anchor and controls address;
- metamorphic testing and simulation validation: provide the cross-domain
  oracle-transfer idea, but not a choice-specific fibre, assignment, or
  estimand.

Direct Scopus and Google Scholar result pages were attempted but are not
accessible in the current connected environment. The search was therefore
cross-checked through ScienceDirect, DOI pages, university repository records
that expose Scopus identifiers, arXiv/repository full text, and exact-title
web-index queries. This is evidence for a targeted boundary audit, not a claim
of a complete subscription-database export. The search found no paper with the
full combination of a candidate-basis fibre, one-member randomized assignment,
zero-gap anchor, and geometry-preserving complexity control.

## Decision

The original broad BMST claim is rejected. The anchored, coarsened-fibre
version is retained as the only main innovation candidate. It passes the
conceptual novelty screen if the paper explicitly enforces the nontrivial
fibre condition, the full-menu condition, the scale/complexity controls, the
power study, and the LR/residual-learning comparison. It remains below full
submission status until the paired DCE and locked simulations are completed.
