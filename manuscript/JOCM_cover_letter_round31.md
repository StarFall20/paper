# Cover letter

Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript “Testing Utility Basis Sufficiency in Discrete Choice Experiments: A Support Complete Fibre Design” for publication in the Journal of Choice Modelling.

The manuscript addresses a design problem in discrete choice experiments. A reduced utility basis can be estimated precisely while the raw attributes removed by that basis remain untestable on the support created by the experiment. We formulate the target as the finite-support relation Y ⟂ X | φ(X), construct feasible non-singleton fibres that preserve the complete candidate menu summary, and provide a support-complete rank and eigenvalue certificate before outcomes are collected.

The contribution is deliberately bounded. The finite-support score is compared with a saturated fibre likelihood ratio, and the paper does not claim a new universal lack-of-fit family. The added design result is a pre-outcome certificate that identifies structural blind directions, separates structural visibility from sample-size power, and returns a support repair when the declared attribute domain cannot expose a direction. The repository includes the exact design calculations, smart-device task-pool audit, finite-support simulations, saturated-LR boundary check, and a public Swissmetro implementation check.

The manuscript is a method-and-design study. It does not report a new questionnaire or claim a causal human-preference effect from the public Swissmetro data. The Swissmetro analysis verifies the implementation and is labelled descriptive. All code and generated outputs are versioned at https://github.com/StarFall20/paper, with a release manifest and a markdown manuscript companion.

This work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has no competing interests to declare. Author and affiliation information can be inserted in the journal submission system.

Thank you for your consideration.
