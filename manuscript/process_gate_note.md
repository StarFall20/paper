# Process-aware triage: innovation audit and prototype

## What is already known

Process heterogeneity is an established choice-modelling topic. Recent JOCM
papers model non-trading and inertia, saliency and attribute non-attendance,
non-compensatory decision rules, and latent-class decision rules. A separate
diagnostic paper uses piecewise regression to identify attribute
non-attendance. A paper that simply adds an ANA, inertia, saliency, or
disjunctive class would overlap that literature and is stopped as a primary
innovation.

## The candidate contribution that remains distinct

The proposed contribution is a **process- and recoverability-aware triage
rule**, not a new ANA or inertia estimator. The rule has four possible
outcomes:

1. enrich the observed utility function;
2. expand to a respondent-heterogeneity model;
3. expand to a process/sequence model; or
4. abstain and request evidence that can distinguish competing mechanisms.

The fourth outcome is essential. A high respondent-level score dispersion can
be generated by continuous taste heterogeneity or by attribute non-attendance.
Choice data alone do not guarantee that those mechanisms are separately
identified. The rule should therefore refuse to label the mechanism when the
available evidence cannot support that distinction. This transfers the reject
option from selective prediction into behavioural model specification, where
the cost of a wrong mechanism is biased WTP or policy simulation.

## Minimal diagnostics

For respondent (n), let (s_{nj}) be the score contribution for candidate
attribute (j) under a pooled MNL and let (I_j) be the corresponding
information contribution. The existing gate uses

\[
Q_j = \frac{\sum_n s_{nj}^2}{\sum_n I_{nj}}.
\]

Values above a bootstrap-calibrated upper limit indicate respondent-level
structure, without identifying whether it is taste or processing. For panel
choices, define a sequence residual

\[
R = \frac{1}{N(T-1)}\sum_{n,t>1}
\left[1\{y_{nt}=y_{n,t-1}\}-\hat P_{nt}(y_{n,t-1})\right],
\]

where (hat P_{nt}) is the independent-task MNL probability. A positive
(R) indicates state dependence or another sequence mechanism omitted from the
utility specification.

The rule is then based on the joint location of (Q_j) and (R), together
with the functional-form diagnostic and candidate-library coverage. The
thresholds must be calibrated on development data and frozen before test
evaluation.

The decision can be written as a selective model-choice problem. Let

\[
\mathcal A = \{\text{enrich},\text{segment},\text{continuous},
\text{process},\text{defer}\}
\]

and let (M) denote the unknown mechanism. The selected action is

\[
\hat a(D)=\arg\min_{a\in\mathcal A}
\widehat{\mathbb E}[L(a,M)\mid D],
\]

where (L) combines policy regret, WTP distortion, predictive loss, model
complexity, and a declared cost for deferring to additional measurement. The
defer action is useful only if its cost is lower than the expected loss of a
forced mechanism label. This makes abstention testable: report its frequency,
the mechanism ambiguity it resolves, and the policy loss avoided.

## Prototype evidence

The exploratory process DGP contains five conditions: additive MNL, random
price sensitivity, price non-attendance, inertia, and a combined
non-attendance/inertia condition. Thirty respondent-grouped replications give
the following development evidence:

| condition | mean (Q_{price}) | mean (R) | (Q>1.30) | (R>0.05) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| additive | 0.982 | -0.001 | 3% | 0% |
| random price sensitivity | 1.773 | 0.014 | 97% | 0% |
| attribute non-attendance | 1.824 | 0.015 | 100% | 0% |
| inertia | 1.578 | 0.161 | 97% | 97% |
| non-attendance + inertia | 2.535 | 0.188 | 100% | 100% |

The useful result is the failure boundary. The respondent gate detects both
random price sensitivity and non-attendance, while the sequence residual
detects inertia. The two zero-sequence conditions remain observationally
confounded. A defensible triage rule must send those cases to an unresolved
branch instead of automatically selecting Mixed Logit or an ANA model.

The raw diagnostics are in
`results/process_gate_30rep.csv`; the grouped summary is in
`results/process_gate_summary_30rep.csv`. These results are exploratory and do
not justify the numerical thresholds in a manuscript.

## Proof required before submission

1. Calibrate (Q) and (R) by a parametric bootstrap under the relevant
   pooled model and report size and power across sample sizes and task counts.
2. Add full comparison branches: enriched MNL, Mixed Logit, latent-class MNL,
   an ANA/process model, and a flexible learner. The comparison must include
   WTP, calibration, choice shares, policy regret, and stability.
3. Include candidate-library omissions and process mechanisms that are absent
   from the library. Report the frequency of abstention and the cost of
   forced decisions.
4. Test the rule on a panel or temporal holdout with meaningful task order. If
   the external data do not contain process information, report the rule as
   unresolved instead of inferring attention or inertia.
5. Distinguish the rule from existing ANA diagnostics and process models in
   the introduction. The novelty is the calibrated, policy-loss-aware
   decision framework and its explicit abstention branch.

If these tests do not reduce policy regret relative to forced model choice,
the paper should retain the recoverability benchmark and drop the process
triage claim.
