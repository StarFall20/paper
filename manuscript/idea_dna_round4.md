# Idea DNA round 4: behavioral metamorphic specification testing

## Candidate

**Behavioral Metamorphic Specification Testing (BMST)** applies the
metamorphic-test-oracle paradigm to discrete-choice utility specification. A
candidate utility basis generates a transformation of a choice task whose
candidate-implied choice probabilities must remain equal. A randomized
held-out task assignment tests that relation.

## DNA

| locus | choice |
|---|---|
| object | a candidate-basis metamorphic relation and its violation estimand |
| intervention | a task transformation preserving all candidate utility differences |
| identification | the softmax equivalence proposition and frozen development candidate |
| validation | additive null, nonlinear/interaction departures, scale and process negative controls |
| output | a basis-sufficiency decision on a declared intervention domain |
| stop rule | report unresolved when scale, carryover, or mixed mechanisms can explain a rejection |

The method transfer is the single innovation. UFIT names the utility-fibre
relation; BMST names the complete testing procedure. MFCA, random forests,
invariant-risk penalties, and policy intervals are comparison or extension
material, not components of the defining object.

## Literature boundary

Metamorphic testing addresses test-oracle construction in software and
simulation validation. Choice-model invariance theory supplies adjacent
behavioral restrictions. The candidate adds a domain-specific relation that is
generated from the fitted utility basis and evaluated with randomized
respondent assignment. The claim remains conditional on a valid paired-task
instrument and a frozen relation.
