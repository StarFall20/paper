# Round eight innovation search: from anchored fibres to nuisance-orthogonal probes

## Purpose

The original manuscript asks when XGBoost outpredicts MNL in a synthetic smart
medical-device benchmark. Recent JOCM work now covers model-assisted
specification, latent-variable neural choice models, process heterogeneity,
reference-dependent neural models, and model-complexity interventions. A
change of algorithm or application domain cannot carry the paper by itself.

The current anchored fibre idea survives the overlap audit, but its first
version still relies on separate zero-gap and geometry controls. This round
asks whether the controls can become one principled method with a stronger
estimand.

## What recent papers make unavoidable

- Flexible DCMs already combine neural representations with theory, and
  constrained networks already enforce monotonicity and improve behavioural
  interpretation.
- Recent JOCM studies manipulate zero prices, cost references, information,
  framing, choice-set size, and decision rules. These interventions can alter
  utility, scale, attention, and comparison difficulty at the same time.
- Bayesian and simulated-annealing designs already optimize parameter
  precision or separation between specified rival models.
- Choice-set confounding, MNL misspecification tests, and standard
  test-retest/monotonicity checks already provide important specification
  diagnostics.

The unresolved methodological problem is narrower: **how can a choice
experiment be designed to test an omitted utility direction after projecting
out the nuisance directions generated by scale, comparison geometry, order,
and task framing?**

## Retained innovation

The retained object is **nuisance-orthogonal utility-fibre testing**. The
existing UFIT relation remains the binary construction; the innovation is the
design and estimand that make the controls mathematically joint.

For task-pair arm \(d\), let \(\Delta(d)\) be the vector of observed choice
probability contrasts between the two candidate-equivalent task versions. Let
\(G_\nu(d)\) be the derivative of that contrast with respect to declared nuisance
parameters, such as error scale drift, comparison-complexity sensitivity,
presentation order, or a framing intercept. Let \(g_\eta(d)\) be the derivative
with respect to the omitted utility direction of interest.

Stacking feasible arms gives \(G_\nu\) and \(g_\eta\). With a positive weight
matrix \(W\), define

\[
P_\nu=G_\nu(G_\nu^\top W G_\nu)^{-1}G_\nu^\top W,\qquad
g_\eta^\perp=(I-P_\nu)g_\eta.
\]

A nuisance-orthogonal contrast \(w\) satisfies \(G_\nu^\top w=0\). Among such
contrasts, choose the one maximizing the standardized projection of
\(g_\eta^\perp\). Equivalently, choose a feasible task subset that maximizes

\[
\frac{g_\eta^\top W(I-P_\nu)g_\eta}
     {\operatorname{Var}\{w^\top\widehat{\Delta}\}}
\]

under respondent burden, attribute-range, dominance, and realism constraints.

The candidate-preserving condition still uses the full menu vector
\(\phi(x)=\{b_j(x)-b_k(x):j<k\}\). The fibre screen remains mandatory. The
zero-gap arm is a special row for which symmetric scale drift has zero first
derivative. A common translation is a special row for which a pairwise-distance
complexity tangent is zero. The proposed method combines these facts into a
single nuisance-orthogonal score.

The primary instrument assigns one member of each pair to each respondent,
freezes the candidate on a development fold, and uses respondent-cluster
sign flips. A mixed signature is reported as unresolved. The method never
claims that the nuisance library is complete; it reports sensitivity to added
nuisance columns.

## Why this is materially different

Classical D-optimal design minimizes parameter variance under a chosen model.
Model-discrimination design maximizes separation between specified rival models.
The proposed criterion maximizes the part of an omitted-utility contrast that
cannot be explained locally by declared nuisance mechanisms. The estimand is a
falsification contrast for a candidate basis, not a better fit, a new
flexible predictor, or a generic model-selection score.

The distinction matters for the existing manuscript. At \(\lambda=1\), XGBoost
can improve predictive metrics without showing whether the improvement is
utility nonlinearity, scale drift, or respondent processing. The new test asks
a narrower question and gives the design an explicit answer.

## Falsification conditions

The method is rejected as a contribution if:

1. the candidate fibre is singleton;
2. the nuisance tangent matrix spans the omitted signal, leaving no power;
3. the selected design only improves D-efficiency;
4. the additive placebo rejects at more than the declared rate;
5. the method loses power when the nuisance library is expanded;
6. the same gains are obtained by an ordinary LR test on the original support;
7. no empirical supplement can implement the randomized pair assignment.

A successful paper must report the rank of \(G_\nu\), the residual signal norm,
condition numbers, design sensitivity to nuisance priors, null size, power,
repair after adding the omitted term, and comparisons with LR, residual
learning, D-optimal, and raw model-discrimination designs.

## Feasible paper story

1. Start from the smart-device benchmark's predictive-model crossover.
2. Show that predictive gains alone do not identify the source of the gain.
3. Define a coarsened burden basis and its nontrivial utility fibres.
4. Derive nuisance-orthogonal candidate-preserving task contrasts.
5. Compare the proposed design with D-optimal, simulated-annealing, fixed
   paired, and ordinary model-discrimination designs.
6. Run omitted utility, random taste, scale, geometry, order, and combined
   mechanisms.
7. Field a forced-choice smart-device supplement or a documented equivalent
   dataset.
8. Report predictive fit, choice shares, WTP, relation violation, nuisance
   sensitivity, and policy consequence together.

## Current assessment

This is the strongest independent upgrade found in the eighth-round search. It
retains the useful fibre construction while replacing an ad hoc collection of
controls with one mathematically defined design criterion. It is still a
proposal until the simulation implementation demonstrates that projection
actually improves size/power under realistic nuisance misspecification.

## First computational audit

The proof-of-concept implementation is in
`analysis/nuisance_orthogonal_fibre_benchmark.py`. It uses 200 replications,
600 respondents, 199 sign flips, five selected focal arms, and a squared
Euclidean geometry index. The L1 distance was removed after an algebraic audit
showed that it is constant on this fixed-sum fibre and gives a zero scale
tangent. With the corrected geometry, the nuisance-orthogonal contrast rejects
at .035 under the null, .750 under the omitted decomposition, .020 under
declared scale, .035 under shared framing, .035 under unlisted nonlinear scale,
.035 under symmetric unlisted complexity, .035 under an out-of-library
complexity interaction, .980 under an out-of-library geometry-framing
interaction, and .385 under the combined condition. The raw-signal comparator
rejects at .025, 1.000, .025, .020, .040, .100, .140, 1.000, and .850. The out-of-library rejection is a deliberate limitation test: the
projection protects against the declared nuisance span and exposes the need to
expand that span when comparison complexity is substantively plausible. The
result is a calibrated planning benchmark, not an empirical application. The
formal local result is in `manuscript/nuisance_orthogonal_proposition.md`; full
design details are in `manuscript/nuisance_orthogonal_fibre_note.md` and the
committed CSV.

## Search boundary and overlap audit

The overlap audit used the following exact-phrase and concept searches in
web-indexed scholarly results: `"utility fibre" discrete choice`,
`"candidate-preserving" discrete choice experiment`,
`"nuisance-orthogonal" discrete choice`, `"metamorphic" discrete choice
model`, `discrete choice specification test omitted utility attribute`, and
`randomized choice experiment utility equivalence transformations`. Searches
restricted to Google Scholar and Scopus domains returned no exact-phrase hit.
The accessible scholarly results did return adjacent work: Chicu and Masten's
choice-set specification test, semiparametric nuisance-orthogonal score
methods, model-discrimination designs, and Shubatt and Yang's tradeoff
complexity theory. The search is evidence against an exact textual duplicate;
it is not a substitute for a logged Scopus export or a citation-network audit.
The final paper must repeat the search in institutional Scopus and Google
Scholar access and archive the query date, filters, and result counts.

The nearest conceptual boundaries are clear. A choice-set specification test
uses changes in the menu to test a monotonicity implication; the present fibre
keeps the full menu-difference vector fixed and changes raw attributes inside a
candidate-equivalence class. A model-discrimination design chooses tasks to
separate named rival models; the present design projects a candidate violation
away from a declared nuisance tangent space. Tradeoff-complexity theory gives a
substantive reason for including geometry and processing columns in that space;
it is a stress-test mechanism, not the proposed estimand.
