# Round-5 cross-literature novelty boundary

## Search result

A targeted cross-literature search used the following combinations:

- metamorphic testing + choice model;
- test oracle + discrete choice specification;
- equivalent choice tasks + utility invariance;
- utility-difference-preserving transformation;
- choice-task invariance + misspecification.

The search found adjacent work in every component and no exact method that
combines a candidate utility basis, a candidate-preserving task relation, a
one-member randomized assignment, and a respondent-cluster sign-flip
specification test for discrete choice. This is a targeted search result, not a
priority claim.

## Adjacent literatures and the surviving delta

| Literature | Established result | Boundary of BMST |
|---|---|---|
| Utility differences and indistinguishability | Choice probabilities depend on utility differences; indistinguishable random-utility representations can share those distributions. Daly (2001) also shows that scale and utility-distribution labels require care. | BMST does not identify latent utility or scale. It tests a declared choice-probability relation on a designed task fibre. |
| Axiomatic conditional logit | Observable translation, presentation, context, and IIA invariances characterize conditional logit under stated assumptions. | BMST operationalizes a fitted candidate basis as a finite experimental test relation. |
| MNL misspecification tests | Pairwise composite likelihood and GMM overidentification tests diagnose IIA/MNL misspecification. | BMST compares different task versions with equal candidate utility differences, rather than alternative subsets within one task. |
| Metamorphic testing | A follow-up input can provide a test oracle when the correct output for one input is unavailable. | BMST transfers this oracle construction into behavioral utility specification. |

## Decision

BMST remains a credible independent method-transfer contribution when the
claim is written at this boundary:

> BMST tests whether observed choice probabilities respect the equivalence
> relation induced by a frozen candidate utility basis on a declared
> intervention domain.

The paper must avoid these broader claims:

- the first invariance test in choice modelling;
- a universal MNL misspecification test;
- identification of a unique nonlinear, interaction, scale, or process
  mechanism;
- a guarantee of external validity outside the tested task domain.

The one-member assignment benchmark and the randomization proposition support
finite-sample testing under the declared assignment. The public Swissmetro
panel still cannot supply the required randomized task relation, so a
paired-task supplement remains the empirical submission gate.
