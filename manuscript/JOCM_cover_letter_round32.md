# Cover letter

Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript “Testing Utility Basis Sufficiency in Discrete Choice Experiments: A Support Complete Fibre Design” for publication in the Journal of Choice Modelling.

The manuscript addresses a design problem in discrete choice experiments. A reduced utility basis can be estimated precisely while the raw attributes removed by that basis remain untestable on the support created by the experiment. We formulate the target as the finite-support relation Y ⟂ X | φ(X), construct feasible non-singleton fibres that preserve the complete candidate menu summary, and provide a support-complete rank and eigenvalue certificate before outcomes are collected.

The contribution is deliberately bounded. The finite-support score is compared with a saturated fibre likelihood ratio; the n=800 500-replication and n=400 1,000-replication checks show the same finite-support rejection boundary for the two procedures. The paper does not claim a new universal lack-of-fit family. The design result identifies structural blind directions, separates structural visibility from sample-size power, and returns a support repair when the declared attribute domain cannot expose a direction.

The manuscript is a method-and-design study. It does not report a new questionnaire or claim a causal human-preference effect from the public Swissmetro data. That analysis is labelled an implementation check. The repository includes the design calculations, smart-device task-pool audit, expanded simulations, overlap audit, validation memo, and release manifest at https://github.com/StarFall20/paper.

This work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has no competing interests to declare. Author and affiliation information can be inserted in the journal submission system.

Thank you for your consideration.
