The working candidate is the exact finite-label formula(5), its calibration-dependent boundary(6), and the frozen low-index nonnull extension(7) in [the derivation](existence_candidate_derivation.md). The risk, Gaussian zonoid, Brier optimality and separation geometry are established ingredients. They enter a single question about finite solutions of one estimator; listing them as multiple innovations would exaggerate the contribution.

The finite formula is a short consequence of the known geometry and a conditional Gaussian law. A reviewer may judge that increment too small. The nonnull Brier-risk boundary, a practically useful treatment of finite auxiliary pools, and a fair calibrated/power-tuned comparison must establish whether the new question changes a choice analyst's decision. Numerical agreement alone cannot answer that question.

The informative-proxy extension adds a sharper candidate: an exact finite-label law where the frozen conditional-mean predictor contains information beyond utility covariates and the population variance-optimal weight tends to one. The existing covariance identity supplies that optimal weight; it is not claimed as our result. The new quantity to assess is the finite-root law at that informative optimum. It avoids relying on inaccurate constant predictions that established power tuning would asymptotically downweight. General sparse surrogate inference and one-step alternatives remain relevant comparisons.

Further full-text inspection of PPI++ §7.1.1 confirms that its logistic experiment already uses independently flipped binary labels as predictions, in dimension2. Its Corollary5 and AppendixB already study one-step PPI. Neither a flipped-label DGP nor taking one Newton step is an innovation here. The planned finite-pool validation uses the released11-dimensional attribute support, freezes a known logit parameter and labels regularized initialization as an explicit comparator adaptation. The exact finite-root distribution and informative-oracle proportional boundary remain the distinct candidate statements.

Targeted query families included prediction-powered/rectified loss with existence, finite minimizer, unboundedness, separation, phase transition, high-dimensional logistic regression and Gaussian lift zonoid; semi-supervised risk prediction and corrected PU risk were traced to primary papers. No exhaustive exclusion of equivalent earlier results is claimed.

The finite-auxiliary derivation explicitly uses [Amelunxen, Lotz, McCoy and Tropp2014, TheoremI and Theorem7.1](https://tropp.caltech.edu/papers/ALMT14-Living-Edge-preprint.pdf). Statistical dimension and the random-nullspace threshold are their established tools. The candidate increment is the estimator-specific, computable joint curve in auxiliary/label ratio, proxy error and positive power. A generic conic reformulation alone would not establish novelty.

Further primary-source screening located [Gu, Kong and Xia2026, §3.4 and AppendixB.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27892v1). They already discuss wide logistic intervals and possible near-separation in PPI comparisons. Their logistic Algorithm5 inverts a score on a parameter grid; the suggested point estimate is a grid score-norm minimizer with theory left open. This rules out claiming the observation of unstable intervals or near-separation as new. The inspected units do not supply the present finite-root phase curve; that limited observation is not an exhaustive priority finding.

[Zhou2026, Theorem5.4 and §5.2–5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25743v1) studies a phase transition in nonparametric squared-error risk as the black-box residual radius varies. Its minimax target differs from finite existence of a specified unpenalized logistic estimator. A title containing “phase transition” neither establishes equivalence nor excludes it without examining the mathematical statement.

[Mani, Xu, Lipton and Oberst2026, §3–4, Proposition4.2 and AppendixA.9.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20178v2), [ICML record](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/mani26b.html), already gives exact finite-sample PPI++ mean-error results, variance costs from estimating weights, a finite-N Gaussian mean case and over-optimistic single-sample variance estimates. A broad “PPI has finite-sample costs” claim is prior work. Their §3 discusses applying score-mean reasoning to M-estimation and explicitly distinguishes the harder nonlinear parameter-error step. The inspected statements do not supply this estimator's logistic-root phase curve; that is a bounded comparison, not a priority certificate.

[Zhang, Zhang and He2026, §2.1–2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32006v1) uses predictive distributions for score calibration and finite-ratio efficiency, with an initial local root and regularity assumptions. Our oracle is not a new score-calibration or efficiency construction. Their inspected results concern consistent calibrated inference, while the present candidate fixes a scalar-power logistic objective and studies finite-root existence as dimension grows proportionally. No blanket comparison against all DiPPI algorithms is inferred.
