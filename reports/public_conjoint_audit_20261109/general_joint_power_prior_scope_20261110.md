# General all-power result: contribution and nearest-prior scope

10 November 2026. This is a bounded applicability review, not an exhaustive Scopus/Google Scholar review or an independent acceptance decision.

The manuscript retains the estimator, Gaussian remaining-attribute regime and frozen low-index prediction model of its fixed-power theorem. The new argument strengthens the quantifiers within that problem: one closed constrained cone controls every power at once; fixed-power thresholds converge uniformly on a localized multiplier set; strict positivity of a zero-scale derivative establishes a gain over ordinary-logit existence. No new behavioral framework or optimal tuning procedure is assembled from these sources.

An additional title-based neighbor was inspected in primary form: [Zhou 2026, Sections 3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25743v1), also [PMLR 306](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/zhou26b.html). It studies squared-loss nonparametric risk around a fixed predictor, a residual-radius minimax transition, and validation-based safe selection. The target, asymptotic parameter and success event differ from finite-root existence of the corrected logit objective. This is a scoped comparison, not evidence that the present result has no predecessor.

The strict-gain statement uses one fixed predictor strictly inside the unit interval. A post-plan analytic counterexample takes the excluded hard predictor g=Z=1−Y. Its scale derivative is nonpositive, the limiting supremum is attained at zero and its boundary equals ordinary logit. Ninety numeric evaluations verify the identity without changing the frozen interior-predictor phase run. The result cannot be generalized by deleting the prediction condition.

The complete numerical grid, independent cone/moment formulations, raw unknown statuses and refined quadrature failure record test calculations. They do not test priority, practical importance or editorial acceptance. The unresolved scientific assessment is whether the full explicit compensated boundary, simultaneous quantifiers and choice-estimation consequences provide enough increment beyond the nearest general results.
