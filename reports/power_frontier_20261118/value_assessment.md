# What this follow-up changes

The empirical relevance question now has an explicit action: compute the power interval on the actual observed menu support. This retains the manuscript's original compensated loss. It does not introduce a second model, new response data, an independent geometry theorem or a universal performance rule.

The follow-up increases finite availability under two specified positive-power conventions, while documenting its forecasting cost and a stronger zero-preserving control. The small-label failures of the original variance rule largely reflect its zero endpoint or a numerically zero feasible upper endpoint. A generic claim that variance tuning is typically repaired by a simple upper cap is unsupported. The newly checked TC-PPI primary source also establishes that quasi-separation in prediction-assisted Bradley–Terry fitting has already been reported.

DiPPI's pooled score framework contains the scalar corrected score under an exact rescaling. Its local regular root result and its categorical-vector construction receive explicit attribution in the proposed revision. The previous direct comparison against a category-only formula rules out a particular substitution, while general conic kinematics remains an inherited tool.

The article has substantial derivations, controlled model experiments, and two transparent external datasets. These are evidence of developed research, not proof of originality or journal acceptance. This follow-up improves the paper's practical interpretation and closes a direct-prior citation gap. It does not on its own establish sufficient JCM methodological value. The next manuscript action is the compact two-prior attribution and a concise frontier consequence, keeping the post-hoc stronger control and adverse comparisons.
