# DH36: analysis conditional on ideal assignment

This exploratory analysis uses all 360 response records, 180 per questionnaire version, retaining all eight choices per record. It conditions on independent records, complete 1:1 respondent-level random assignment and no interference. These assumptions define the analysis scenario; they are not verified recruitment facts. Original provenance is preserved. No claim that all records are verified human responses is made.

The candidate map combines the fixed profile levels as (D+S, I+E), retaining prices, sharing and the outside alternative. All eight paired menus preserve the complete candidate representation. There are 16 supported raw menus, eight two-point fibres, eight independent raw-menu contrasts and 16 independent categorical-probability contrasts. These are finite-support lack-of-fit dimensions. They are not a new testing principle or a guarantee of power beyond this support.

The studentized 16-dimensional cluster Wald statistic is 28.0468. Complete respondent-vector permutation with 999,999 draws gives p=0.048687 (Monte Carlo 95% interval [0.048266, 0.049110]). The complete-fibre pooled score has exactly the same permutation ordering under balanced allocation. The saturated task-by-version composite deviance gives p=0.048172. Whole-vector exchangeability provides finite-sample exactness; marginal equality alone supports the studentized test only asymptotically under regularity conditions. The ordinary independent-choice LR reference is not used.

A frozen random-forest residual direction trained on 240 records gives a one-sided heldout p=0.2022 on 120 records. The heldout logscore gain is -0.00522 and its descriptive interval includes zero. Full-sample and heldout tests have different analysis sizes; these results do not establish power dominance.

The common MNL price coefficient is -0.2115 per CNY 10 (cluster interval [-0.2379, -0.1851]). A random service-participation intercept improves five-fold joint response prediction. Joint negative log score divided by eight is 1.041854 for the MNL product, 0.983397 for the common random-intercept model and 0.981693 with version constants. The incremental version gain is 0.00170 per task, with a descriptive interval spanning zero. All eight answers are held out together. Every training fold uses the same three fixed initial values, independently of heldout outcomes. An expanded random-price model reached the zero-variance boundary; the final model fixes that variance to zero. Quadrature at 101 and 161 nodes yields coefficient differences below 3e-8.

Calibration uses 5,000 independent datasets in each of three null/dependence scenarios and 999 complete-record permutations per dataset. Prospective power uses hypothetical probability shifts, independent 5,000-null-dataset cutoffs and 1,000 datasets per cell. It is not observed power or a universal sample-size recommendation. At n=360, an eight-task A/B probability shift of five percentage points has simulated power 0.458, while an eight-percentage-point shift has power 0.900, conditional on the stated calibration.

For the marginal null with arbitrary within-record dependence, exact conditional 2x3 tests were enumerated separately for each task and combined using Bonferroni/Holm. The global Bonferroni p-value is 0.105614; no task passes familywise correction. This check is finite-sample valid under independent records and the taskwise common-probability null, without joint vector exchangeability. The omnibus studentized permutation test rejects in 0.0612 of 5,000 unequal-dependence weak-null simulations, so its asymptotic robustness is not treated as a finite-sample guarantee at n=360. The global permutation and exact marginal tests have different finite-sample assumptions and are both disclosed.

The evidence is borderline under the stronger joint exchangeability scenario and is not consistently supported in heldout data. Marginal sufficiency failure has not been confirmed by the finite-sample-valid check. The raw attributes are bundled by version, so their individual effects and competing framing, complexity or scale mechanisms are not separately identified. Equal-weight sums of attribute codes require an application-based justification independent of these outcomes. The results do not establish originality, superiority over classical lack-of-fit methods, human provenance, actual random allocation or readiness for Journal of Choice Modelling submission.

Permutation inference follows the distinction in [Chung and Romano (2013)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.5939); panel mixed logit follows [Train](https://emlab.berkeley.edu/books/choice2.html). Public aggregates reproduce the observed Wald statistic, but exact respondent-level permutations and integrated panel likelihoods require controlled access to the original response vectors. Raw exports and participant-level responses are not included in this release.
