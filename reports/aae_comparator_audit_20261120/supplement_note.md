# Comparison with all-soft choice estimation

Wang et al. (2026, equation 3) estimate human choice probabilities and use them as soft labels in an auxiliary-menu log likelihood. Their empirical implementation pools the primary and auxiliary menus in the second stage. Let $L_S(g;\beta)$ denote the negative log likelihood on menu set $S$ with forecast probabilities as labels. Let $\Delta$ denote the labelled average observed choice feature moment minus its predicted value. Normalization of each probability vector gives
$$Q_\lambda(\beta)=(1-\lambda)L_L(g;\beta)+\lambda L_U(g;\beta)-\Delta^T\beta.$$
At unit power, the all-soft part is auxiliary-only. At $\lambda=N/(m+N)$, it gives equal weight to every pooled menu. On the same pooled menus, retaining human labels in the labelled part applies the residual multiplier $m/(m+N)$. The pooled all-soft criterion and the full correction apply multipliers zero and one. These three losses share the log partition function.

All-soft and corrected losses agree at matched weights if $\Delta=0$. A finite unpenalized same-sample first-stage Logit that includes the focal attribute directions satisfies this condition through its score equations. The equality is conditional on the predictions used in the objectives. Its implication for inference depends on how the probabilities are learnt. Van der Laan and van der Laan (2026, Section 3.1) derive a related exact representation for mean calibration. Lee and Kim (2026, Section 4) study conditional-score calibration for first-order efficiency.

When forecasts assign positive probability to every alternative and the weighted contrasts span the parameter space, the all-soft moment $b_0$ lies inside the menu support $K$. With $\Delta\ne0$, define $a_*$ as the last feasible residual multiplier along $b_0+a\Delta$. It is the minimum of $h_K(d)-b_0^Td$ subject to $\Delta^Td=1$, where $h_K(d)$ is the support function of $K$. A finite root exists for $0\leq a<a_*$. At equality the target lies on the boundary. For $a>a_*$ a supporting direction has recession slope $a_*-a$. This supplies a direct comparison of the two matched-weight objectives.

We fixed the first-stage forecasts on the same 500 allocations per budget from the public binary choice archive. Both all-soft objectives returned finite fits in every allocation. At 50 labelled choices with AI predictions, the full correction on the pooled menus was exterior in five allocations. With forecasts based only on attributes, it was exterior in two. Among the 495 shared finite AI fits, full correction raised log loss by 0.19477 on the fixed human holdout. The corresponding difference at 150 labelled choices was 0.01871 across all 500 allocations. The comparison implements the second-stage equations with shared cross-fitted probabilities. The first-stage model, allocation protocol and evaluation loss define the scope of these estimates.
