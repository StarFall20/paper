### Matching derivatives at the true choice coefficient

The result also holds for an informative predictor that equals the true choice probability. Fix true intercept zero and true slope $\log 2$. At integer contrast $x$, use $g(x)=2^x/(1+2^x)$. Let the available signed contrasts be symmetric pairs with positive integer magnitudes $u$. Write $L(t)=\log(1+\exp t)$, and pair the two signs at each magnitude. At the true coefficient, the prediction-loss score is zero on every row. For derivative order $r\geq2$ and $j$ slope coordinates, the pair contributes zero when $r+j$ is odd. When $r+j$ is even, the pair contributes $u^jL^{(r)}(u\log 2)$.

For any given maximum order $K$, include the constant function and all these nonzero derivative functions through $K$. The auxiliary contribution to positive-slope recession is governed by another function, $s(u)=u/(1+2^u)$. This function is independent of the derivative constraints on the positive integers. To establish independence, set $z=2^{-u}$. The function $L^{(2)}(u\log 2)$ equals $z/(1+z)^2$. Each further derivative acts by $-z$ times differentiation with respect to $z$, so the rational function for order $r$ has a pole of exact order $r$ at $z=-1$. A putative relation can be multiplied by a power of $1+z$ to become a polynomial in $z$ with coefficients polynomial in $u$. A nonzero lowest coefficient cannot be cancelled as the integers $u$ increase, because higher powers of $2^{-u}$ decay exponentially. Thus any relation also holds with $u$ and $z$ as free variables. Comparing powers of $u$ and then pole orders shows that all coefficients vanish. In particular the coefficient of $u$ in $s(u)$ has a simple pole, while positive derivative orders starting at two only supply poles of higher, distinct orders.

Choose a finite set of magnitudes including 1 and 2 on which these rational vectors have full rank. The two evaluations at 1 and 2 are independent because $s$ takes different values there. Extend them using the full rank on the positive integers. Start from uniform magnitude probabilities $f_0$. A rational frequency direction can preserve all included derivative constraints and change the mean of $s$. Take equal positive and negative steps small enough to keep every cell probability positive. The two steps and the original frequencies share the same signed menus.

Use correction weight $1/2$. Let $m_L$ be the mean magnitude among the labelled choices and let $s_L$ and $s_0$ be the mean of $s$ for the labelled and starting auxiliary designs. Take the labelled frequencies halfway between $f_0$ and the magnitude with the highest $s$, so $s_L>s_0$. Choose a constant labelled choice-one fraction on the positive contrasts and its complement on the negative contrasts, giving slope moment $m_L/2-(s_L-s_0)/2$. This moment is strictly between zero and $m_L/2$, so both human choices occur at every signed contrast. All design and label fractions are rational and admit common integer totals across the three designs.

The intercept moment is $1/2$, and symmetry minimizes the intercept at zero for any fixed slope. For an auxiliary design with mean $s_U$, the positive-slope recession cost is $(s_U-s_0)/2$. The negative-slope cost is positive because the fitted support in either direction has positive extent and the corrected slope moment is positive. The three designs give an unbounded objective, an unattained finite infimum and a unique finite minimum. They preserve the labelled rows, the forecast and all loss derivatives of positive order through $K$ at the true utility coefficient. Exact rational computations verify orders one through nine. These constructs establish possibility. Each design and its fixed labels has positive probability when both menu samples are drawn from one common full-support law and human choices are generated from $g$.
