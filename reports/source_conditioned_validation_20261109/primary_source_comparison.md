# Primary literature and GitHub comparison

The comparisons concern the current source-pattern candidate. They do not certify worldwide priority or constitute a completed Scopus/Google Scholar review.

| Primary source and reading level | Relevant established work | Treatment in this project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Kazagli and de Lapparent, JOCM 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jocm.2023.100413); author-repository full text, Section 2 including equation (8) | Represents a deterministic non-trading rule in a finite mixture and models contextual variation in decision-rule membership. | Constant-choice vectors and membership variation are established ingredients. The diagnostic hurdle is not introduced as new behavioral theory. |
| [Mariel and Artabe, Resource and Energy Economics 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.reseneeco.2025.101496); primary full text, Sections 2 and 5 | Uses fitted random-parameter logit simulations to assess excess frequencies of selected choice patterns; the test does not determine their cause. | Complete pattern counts and the source-conditional predictive comparison are retained. No claim is made to have replicated the authors' test or explained why records are constant. |
| [Alcorta and Mariel, JOCM 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jocm.2026.100620); publisher abstract and highlights | Derives a first-order approximation to MNL bias from omitted individual-variable/ASC interactions. | The source/ASC ordinary model is a necessary substantive comparator here, with no separate methodological novelty claim. |
| [Official publisher journal description](https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/journal-of-choice-modelling/1755-5345); description section | Welcomes methodological contributions and innovative applications, across choice-modeling domains and survey design. | The topic concerns the readership. Scope fit does not prove the current case has adequate independent contribution. A new theorem is not treated as a universal requirement. |

The 2025 article explicitly links [discretechoice/simulation-based-method](https://github.com/discretechoice/simulation-based-method). Its root listing, README and both figure scripts were inspected at commit `7cec56ba9d2a8b28a48e370df4693bf552a9a488`. The README retains an earlier 2024 working title; the published article is dated 2025. The scripts make the tested vectors, block-specific observed frequencies and simulation seed explicit. These are useful provenance and denominator conventions. No source code or data from the repository was copied into the present package, and no execution or successful replication of those R scripts is claimed.

The present paper-specific difference is an observed perfect association between the export-source group and constant-vector status, followed by measured source-specific prediction errors hidden by an accurate pooled count. The design does not identify why that association occurs. This supports a focused exploratory case note; a distinct submission-ready innovation is still unproven.
