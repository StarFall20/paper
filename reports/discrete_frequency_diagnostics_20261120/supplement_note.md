## Appendix J Identical discrete profiles with different frequencies

The diagnostic limitation persists when every sample uses the same discrete profiles. Fit an intercept and a slope of $x$ and keep the predictor $g(x,z)=1/2+z/4$ and correction weight $1/2$ fixed. Each sample has an equal number of rows at the two informative profiles $(0,-1)$ and $(0,1)$. Together these make up half the sample. The other rows have $z=0$ and occur in opposite-sign pairs with magnitudes $0,1,2,3$. The pair counts are $(2,2,6,10)$ in the labelled sample. On the positive sides of the four pairs the counts for choice one are $(1,1,5,9)$, with complementary counts on the negative sides. At the informative profiles the choice fractions are $1/4$ and $3/4$. Both outcomes occur at all nine profiles in the 80 labelled rows.

The 144 auxiliary rows use pair counts $(1,31,1,3)$, $(11,16,7,2)$ or $(21,1,13,1)$. Every profile occurs in every design. The count difference $(-10,15,-6,1)$ annihilates the powers $1,u^2,u^4$ on these magnitudes. Thus all partial derivatives through order five at zero agree. The corrected moment is $(1/2,2/5)^T$, and each score row at zero agrees after matching row order. The profiled intercept remains zero. The exact positive-slope recession costs are $1/48$, zero and $-1/48$. The largest feasible weights in the three designs are $18/31$, $1/2$ and $18/41$. The first design gives a unique finite estimate, the second gives a finite unattained infimum and the third gives an objective unbounded below. Ordinary labelled Logit has a finite estimate. Enumerating positive integer pair counts at the fixed sample size recovers exactly these three possibilities from their common moments. The sixth slope derivative distinguishes them.

This gives a sharp information limit for moment diagnostics in this structured family. With $L$ known distinct magnitudes, the even moments through order $2(L-1)$ identify all pair frequencies, including the absolute moment that determines recession. The moment matrix in the squared magnitudes is Vandermonde. At zero, the even slope derivatives multiply the weighted even feature moments by known nonzero log-partition derivatives. The labelled moments are fixed. With fewer even moments and all frequencies positive, choose a subset of $k+2$ magnitudes if moments through order $2k$ are observed. The barycentric weights on their squared magnitudes sum to zero against every polynomial of degree at most $k$. Their product with the magnitudes sums to a nonzero divided difference of the square root. Small changes in either direction preserve all supplied moments and support and change the recession cost. For rational magnitudes and frequencies the changes can be made rational and expanded into equal-weight samples. At a fixed sample size, integer counts may constrain the possible changes further. This argument uses classical moment matching as in Cai and Low (2011). The added restriction here is common discrete profile support at a fixed correction weight.
