# Proposed manuscript wording

## Main text after the finite-power discussion

A finite fit can give poor choice probabilities well before correction reaches its upper feasible power. We trace eight powers for every positive upper endpoint below one in the food DCE. All 107 endpoints have a single supporting facet. At five labelled respondents, moving from half to nine tenths of the endpoint raises median evaluation log loss from 1.3075 to 2.2208 across 87 allocations. The median paired increase is 0.8067. The same power movement changes the median from 0.9004 to 0.9264 in the 356 allocations with an interior unit-power estimate. All fits exist at both points. The fitted utility comparison at a GBP 2.50 Bere premium changes in six of the 87 allocations. Price coefficients remain negative along all evaluated paths.

At an endpoint below one, the finite coefficient diverges along the outward normal to the supported menus' facet. Its leading rate is the logarithm of inverse distance to the endpoint divided by the smallest positive normal utility gap. The remaining coefficients converge in the subspace identified by the alternatives tied on the facet. The same gap calculation gives the leading increase in log loss for an evaluation choice outside that menu's tied set. In all 107 observed cases at least one evaluation choice has a positive gap. The boundary diagnoses loss of estimability. A forecast check shows how choice probabilities behave before the boundary is reached.

## Placement and scope

Introduce the fixed-design result after Proposition 1 or in the empirical appendix, with Geyer (2009) and Rinaldo, Fienberg and Zhou (2009) cited for the conditional model and its normal. Keep the proof, the two budget denominators, all regular endpoint controls and the conditional price ratio quantities in the supplement. The all-gap scalar comparison and its finite error bound are post-hoc and belong in the technical evidence. At the 90% point the scalar approximation is not accurate enough to replace the full likelihood fit. This analysis uses an existing choice loss and the same archived respondents, prediction tool and evaluation set.
