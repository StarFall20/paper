### Matching arbitrary finite orders on common support

For any integer $k\geq0$, take the $k+2$ magnitudes $1,\ldots,k+2$, with equal mass on the two signs of each magnitude. Let $p_0$ be the uniform distribution over magnitudes. Perturb its weights by a small positive or negative multiple of the barycentric weights on the squared magnitudes. All three frequency vectors have positive entries. They agree in the even moments through order $2k$ and differ in the mean magnitude. This follows from the divided difference of the square root at distinct positive points.

Use correction weight $1/2$ and the same constant forecast $g=1/2$ in both samples. Take labelled magnitude frequencies halfway between $p_0$ and a point mass at the largest magnitude, with the signs still balanced. Let the mean magnitudes be $\mu_L$ in the labelled design and $\mu_0$ under $p_0$. Then $\mu_L>\mu_0$. Set the label choice-one fraction on every positive contrast to $1/2+(\mu_L+\mu_0)/(4\mu_L)$ and use its complement on each negative contrast. Both fractions lie strictly between zero and one. Positive rational frequencies and fractions can be realized at common integer auxiliary and labelled totals across the three designs. All samples use the same signed contrasts.

The corrected intercept moment is $1/2$ and the slope moment is $(\mu_L+\mu_0)/4$. The intercept minimizes the objective at zero for each fixed slope by symmetry. The two signed slope directions have recession costs $(\mu_U-\mu_0)/4$ and $(2\mu_L+\mu_U+\mu_0)/4$, where $\mu_U$ is the mean auxiliary magnitude. The three auxiliary designs put the positive slope cost on either side of zero and at zero. They give a unique finite minimum, a finite unattained infimum and an objective unbounded below. The gradients at zero coincide. The even-order partial derivatives through $2k$ depend only on the matched feature moments, and the higher odd derivatives at zero vanish. The reproducibility check verifies the construction by exact rational arithmetic for orders 1 through 33. A common distribution assigning positive mass to all profiles and generating independent Bernoulli choices with mean $1/2$ gives positive probability to each finite design and its labels.
