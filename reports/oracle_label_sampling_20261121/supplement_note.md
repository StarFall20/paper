### Exact probabilities with random human choices

The preceding construction fixes the observed responses. We next fix the labelled and auxiliary menu frequencies and draw each human response independently from the true probability $g(x)=2^x/(1+2^x)$. The auxiliary forecasts and correction weight $1/2$ stay fixed. For each of derivative orders one through four, we use the three preceding frequency designs. The corrected gradient at the true coefficient agrees for every realization of the labels because the auxiliary prediction loss has zero gradient there. The higher derivatives still agree as specified by the construction.

Write $n_x$ for the number of labelled menus at signed contrast $x$, $m=\sum_x n_x$, $h_x=n_x/m$, and $a_x$ for the auxiliary share. The two observed sums are $S=\sum_iY_i$ and $T=\sum_ix_iY_i$. Their joint probability mass is read from the coefficients of $\prod_x(1-g(x)+g(x)u v^x)^{n_x}$. For each contrast $c$, the two normals $\pm(-c,1)$ characterize the edges of the attainable moment polygon. The sign of the smallest slack across these normals classifies a realization as interior, boundary or exterior. We compute the joint probabilities of the three corrected fit states by summing the corresponding probability masses, conditional on ordinary Logit attaining a finite solution. The verifier checks the classification against independently enumerated polygons for 2,112 comparisons and checks four joint distributions against all their binary response vectors.

We evaluate balanced labelled menus and menus that place half their probability on the magnitude with largest $|x|(1-g(|x|))$ and distribute the other half uniformly across magnitudes. Each pattern uses four integer replications of its smallest symmetric labelled design. There are 32 settings. The largest conditional probability of a finite corrected fit under one frequency design and an unbounded objective under another is $2.61\times 10^{-4}$. It occurs with eight human choices at order one. The maxima for orders two, three and four are $6.53\times10^{-15}$, $3.07\times10^{-6}$ and $2.59\times10^{-6}$. For comparison, at order four with 36 labels and the concentrated pattern, the probability that at least one objective is unbounded is 0.0585. The finite-to-unbounded switch in that setting remains rare. The high-order designs use rational frequency weights, and their equivalent integer auxiliary counts can be large. These are conditional probabilities for the constructed distributions. The public-data comparisons below describe observed choices and deliberately changed frequencies.
