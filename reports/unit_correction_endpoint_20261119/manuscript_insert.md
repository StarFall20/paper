### C17 Continuation to unit correction

Let $s=1-\lambda$, $H=Q_0$, $U=Q_1$, and $h=H^\infty$. The criterion is $(1-s)U+sH$. The auxiliary menus alone supply log partitions in $U$. Assume the combined within-menu contrasts span the coefficient space. A direction with negative unit recession excludes finite estimation sufficiently close to unit correction.

For a feasible unit target, find the tied auxiliary alternatives $T_i$ in its minimal support face as in C13. Their within-menu differences span a space $E$. Write $N$ for its orthogonal complement. For an outside auxiliary alternative define $a_{ij}=\Pi_N(x_i^*-x_{ij})$, with $x_i^*$ taken from $T_i$. Unit recession vanishes on exactly the directions $d$ in $N$ with $a_{ij}^Td\geq0$ for every outside alternative. To see this, represent the unit target with positive mass on all the tied alternatives. The recession sum is zero exactly when every one of these alternatives maximizes its menu's directional utility. Because $h\geq0$, the support criterion gives a unique finite estimate for every $0<s<1$ exactly when $h$ is strictly positive on every nonzero direction of zero unit recession.

If every auxiliary alternative is tied, $U$ attains its minimum on an affine space. As $s$ decreases to zero the estimate selects the minimizer of $H$ on that space. Optimality bounds $H$ by its value at the selected point and sends $U$ to its minimum. The fitted component in $E$ then converges by strict convexity and coercivity of the unit fit restricted to $E$. Positivity of $h$ on the unit directions of $N$ bounds the remaining component and makes the selected minimizer unique.

For a proper auxiliary face, set $L=\log(1/s)$. Minimize $h(v)$ over $v$ in $N$ with $a_{ij}^Tv\geq1$. The objective is a sum of labelled menu-wise maxima and has a linear epigraph formulation. The first optimal set is compact because $h$ is strictly positive on the unit directions of zero unit recession. In each labelled menu pick a maximizing alternative $r_i$ at a relative interior point of this set. It maximizes utility throughout the set because the sum of the convex label maxima is affine there and each individual maximum must be affine. Define the labelled gaps as $(x_{ir_i}-x_{ij})^Tv$. Starting with the first optimal set, successively maximize the minimum among the still-varying labelled gaps and the still-varying auxiliary gaps $a_{ij}^Tv-1$. Retain the maximizing set after each step. The joint contrasts span the coefficient space, so the final set is a singleton $v_*$. Then $\widehat\beta_s=L v_*+O(1)$.

For the proof, comparison with fixed trial coefficients gives $U(\widehat\beta_s)\to\inf U$. The tied-face objective gives convergence of the component in $E$. The projected unit score is a weighted sum of outside probabilities times their gaps, and is of order $s$ because the labelled score is bounded. An exposing direction makes all outside probabilities $O(s)$. For the normal component $n_s$, log probability ratios then give $a_{ij}^Tn_s\geq L-O(1)$. Comparison with $Lv$ for a first-stage minimizer, using the bounded difference between labelled log loss and $h$, bounds $h(n_s)$ above by the first optimum times $L$ plus $O(1)$. The linear error bound used in C13 puts $n_s$ within $O(1)$ of the dilated first optimal set.

At later stages hold the bounded remainder and the component in $E$ fixed. The labelled affine terms cancel when comparing points in the first optimal set. After dividing the objective by $s$, a varying labelled gap $b$ contributes at order $\exp(-Lb)$. A varying auxiliary gap $a$ contributes at order $\exp(-L(a-1))$. Factoring out the constant gaps in each positive log sum gives uniform upper and lower bounds by sums of these exponentials. A comparison at the maximum of the smallest varying gap forces the same gap at the optimizer to agree up to $O(1/L)$. The linear error bound again yields an $O(1)$ distance to the new dilated set. Each step reduces dimension, which proves the claimed direction. The leading log-loss coefficient on an evaluation menu is $\max_j(x_j-x_y)^Tv_*$.

In a constructed three-alternative example, the first optimal set is $v=(1,z)$ with $-1\leq z\leq1$. The two gap families together select $z=0$. The labelled family alone selects $1/2$ and the auxiliary family alone selects $-1$. The original corrected objective gives a finite second-coefficient limit of $-\log(3)/2$ and a limiting loss of $\log(1+1/\sqrt{3})$ when the evaluation contrast is the second attribute and the outside option is chosen.
