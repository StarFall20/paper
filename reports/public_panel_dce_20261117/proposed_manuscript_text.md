## Respondent-separated audit on released food choices

We also audit a released Scottish consumer DCE on shortbread products (Chen et al., 2025). The source contains 299 respondent records. We retain the 298 respondents with six complete menus, giving 1,788 observed choices among two products and a neither option. A fixed respondent split assigns 100 people to predictor training, 60 to evaluation and 138 to the correction pool. Each person's six choices remain together throughout. The target is an eleven-parameter marginal conditional logit with product-position constants, categorical ingredient, nutrition, quality-label, access and origin effects, and linear price. An independently trained ridge logit adds age and education interactions to generate frozen probabilities. No synthetic attributes or responses are added.

We fix all model choices before fitting. Each of 500 random permutations of the correction pool supplies nested labelled groups of 5, 10, 20 and 40 respondents; the remaining people supply auxiliary menus with their choices withheld. The finite-design support check retains a simplex for every menu. Repeated designs are combined by adding their exact weights. This is a diagnostic on the observed design. It imposes no Gaussian attribute law or independence of choices within a person.

At five labelled respondents, ordinary MNL has an interior target in 319 of 500 allocations. The compensated objective has 356 finite fits at unit power and 426 at power one half. Unit power has an exterior target in 144 allocations, including 40 in which ordinary MNL is interior. At ten labelled respondents the corresponding unit-power counts are 479 finite fits and 21 exterior targets; 18 exterior cases have an interior ordinary target. All methods have finite fits at twenty and forty labelled respondents. These results locate the observed failure in the smallest label budgets. They do not estimate its frequency in other DCE populations.

The cluster-score power comparison uses the established sandwich trace criterion with a common ridge pilot and fully separated labelled and auxiliary respondents (Broska and Howes, 2026). It has 352 and 495 finite fits at five and ten labelled respondents. At five people, 136 boundary or numerically near-boundary cases select zero power and return to ordinary MNL. The criterion supplies no general finite-attainment guarantee. Our pilot convention differs from the unit-power start described in that work; this comparison evaluates a stated formula implementation.

Held-out probability losses retain unavailable fits and common-availability denominators. With a labelled-ridge fallback, unit-power correction has mean human log loss 1.0787 at five labelled respondents, compared with 0.9081 for labelled ridge. Their paired difference is 0.1706 with allocation Monte Carlo standard error 0.0414. We also charge the predictor's one hundred training respondents to the human budget. Ordinary MNL using those labels and the correction labels has lower held-out loss than the corrected fits at all four budgets. The empirical contribution is a respondent-separated illustration of finite-attainment failure and its limits, with training costs and adverse comparisons retained.

All Monte Carlo statements condition on the fixed released respondents, trained predictor and sixty-person evaluation set. They describe random label allocation. They provide neither fresh-population uncertainty nor a validation of the proportional Gaussian boundary.
