# Cover letter

Dear Editors,

Please consider When prediction assisted logit estimates exist for publication in the Journal of Choice Modelling.

The paper studies finite estimation for an existing prediction-corrected logit objective. We derive explicit existence boundaries when the utility dimension grows with the human label budget, retaining finite auxiliary samples, utility signal and fitted choice constants. A controlled comparison preserves every realized truth score and the full fixed dimensional covariance while finite-solution probabilities converge to opposite limits. The same boundary explains how increasing auxiliary data along an established variance power rule can remove a finite estimate, and how vanishing positive powers can recover roots with diverging human probability loss.

The analysis covers binary contrasts and common-coefficient choice menus. An extension admits independent symmetric discrete noise profiles while retaining the Gaussian signal. Classical support geometry, conic results and universality supply the tools. The contribution concerns the explicit compensated boundary and its consequences for choice estimation.

Released vaccine choices connect the theory to realized designs through exact support audits, nested auxiliary budgets and a held-out-record diagnostic. Comparisons include ridge, Firth, pooling and established prediction-assisted estimators. They retain adverse findings: higher availability does not ensure better forecasts. A fixed total label experiment also counts predictor training costs. These results give choice analysts separate evidence about existence, probability quality and uncertainty. The reproduction archive provides a direct table-to-source map, fixed plans, provenance and executable checks.

The manuscript addresses methodological choice analysis at the interface with machine-assisted estimation. It uses existing released observations. This research received no funding, and the author declares no competing interests.
