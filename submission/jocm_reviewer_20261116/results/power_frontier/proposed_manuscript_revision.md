## Section 4.6 or its linked empirical appendix, compact consequence

A follow-up keeps all 2000 food-DCE allocations and computes their feasible power intervals by linear programming. Choosing half the upper endpoint gives 443, 499, 500 and 500 finite fits at the four respondent budgets; unit power gives 356, 479, 500 and 500. Projecting the cluster-variance power into the central 80% of each interval gives the same availability. In the five-person group, its all-allocation fallback log loss is 1.3043, compared with 0.9081 for labelled ridge. A post-hoc upper-cap control preserves zero powers; it gives 353 finite fits and loss 1.1644 in that group, close to the original variance rule. Direct human MNL using the predictor-training labels has lower loss at every budget. Computing an admissible power interval supports a concrete fitting decision; forecast assessment and label costs remain consequential.
