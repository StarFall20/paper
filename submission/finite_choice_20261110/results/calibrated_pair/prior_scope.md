# Read primary statements and remaining priority questions

Checked on 13 November 2026. This records bounded primary-source readings. Scopus and Google Scholar result pages were inaccessible; a completed systematic database review is not claimed. No absence of a search hit establishes originality.

[van der Laan and van der Laan, “Calibeating” Prediction-Powered Inference, v2 dated 12 November 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21260v2), Sections 3–4 and the post-processing efficiency statements, already establish calibration and efficient use of transformed scores for semisupervised mean estimation. The identities relating calibrated residual risk to regular efficiency are established. Here two different calibrated signals match conditional second moments; their compensated logit attainment boundaries differ. The example is not a new calibration method or a contradiction of fixed-dimensional efficiency.

[Chen, Bai and Zhu, Covariance-Calibrated Prediction-Powered Inference, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21837v2), Section 4.2 and Supplement S3.10 Proposition S3.3, already separate regular equivalence from finite-sample fitting and give a sufficient observable global certificate for their complete GMM criterion. Their structured logistic risk result is conditional on finite pilot existence. The present calculation concerns the scalar compensated loss with frozen calibrated predictors. A difference between local covariance and finite behavior is already known; the candidate increment must be the explicit matched-conditional-moment choice boundary and its consequences. The complete-GMM alternative remains in the main manuscript and is not assumed to share the compensated loss's failure event.

[Zhang, Zhang and He, Beyond Point Predictions: Distribution-Informed Prediction-Powered Inference](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32006v1), Sections 2 and Appendix B.1, already use predictive distributions to improve score-level efficiency and prove a local root by contraction under their regularity conditions. Appendix A.2 matches a predictive distribution's mean and variance while varying outcome-relevant skewness. The present binary posterior is fully specified by its success probability; the variation here is the population distribution of that posterior across rows, with complete regular score covariance held equal. No broad claim that distributional information beyond two moments is new is justified.

[Sargent, Sun, Zhang and Liu, PP-Rank](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13186v1), Sections 4.2–4.3, already analyze preference correction and action-specific variance allocation. Their Theorem 1 proves a bounded approximate-root sequence under the stated ranking/sampling conditions. A claim that preference PPI only assumes existence would misstate it. Their supplementary proofs and implementations have not received a complete equivalence audit in this follow-up, and no benchmark superiority is asserted.

[Götze, Kabluchko and Zaporozhets](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.04184v2), Theorem 3.1 and Lemma 5.1, directly supply the arbitrary-set Gaussian absorption machinery after conditioning on low-dimensional indices. [Koriyama and Bellec](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/koriyama25a.html) supply general Gaussian M-estimator existence theory with an absolute row-infimum assumption in the inspected formulation. The manuscript already credits these results. An objective-specific evaluation and a carefully matched counterexample can be a derived contribution; they do not create a new absorption or separation principle.
